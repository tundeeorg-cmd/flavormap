# Figure 2 — replacement specification (**draft, not adopted**)

CLAUDE.md §6 specifies Figure 2 as a distance-decay scatter: ~2,900 province pairs, km on
X, cosine distance on Y, LOESS fit with the change point marked. At a 1.3% labelled
fraction the unit collapses to four regions and that figure has **six points**. It cannot
be built. This is a draft replacement for the researcher to accept, amend or reject; §6
is unchanged until then.

Constraints inherited unchanged: 2D (rule 10), 300 dpi, colourblind-safe, `matplotlib` +
`seaborn`, regenerated by `make figures` from the database, never hand-edited (rule 5).

## The design problem

The original figure had one job: show that composition varies with distance, and mark
where it breaks. The rebuilt analysis (`docs/rq1_region_level.md`) found something the
original spec has no way to draw — **all of the regional signal sits on a single boundary,
and none of it tracks distance**. Three Northeast pairs separate (Holm-adjusted p = 0.0006
to 0.0012); the three pairs among North, Central and South do not (Holm 0.25 to 0.83), and
two of those three have n under 25, so they are undetermined rather than null.

A figure that showed only the six separations would let a reader treat the three
non-significant pairs as measured zeros. A figure that showed only significance would hide
that the effect is confined to one region. Both facts have to be in the frame.

## Panel A — separation against geographic distance

| | |
|---|---|
| **X** | great-circle km between region centroids. Six values |
| **Y** | compositional separation: mean between-group minus mean within-group cosine distance on recipe TF-IDF |
| **Marks** | one point per region pair, six total, each labelled with the pair (`NE–N`, `C–S`, …) |
| **Error** | vertical bar spanning the 2.5th–97.5th percentile of that pair's permutation null, 9,999 permutations, seed 42 |
| **Fill** | filled = Holm-adjusted p < 0.05; hollow = not significant |
| **Annotation** | n beside each point — the three non-significant pairs are the three smallest, and the reader must be able to see that |

**No fitted line. No LOESS. No change point. No boundary width in kilometres.** Six points
cannot carry a fit, and drawing one would reintroduce exactly the claim the analysis
withdrew. The absence is the argument, so the caption states it rather than leaving the
reader to notice.

What the panel shows: whether the separated pairs are the distant ones. If the significant
pairs are not the far ones, then compositional difference is not a function of distance at
this resolution — which is an answer to RQ1, and a more interesting one than a decay curve.
**The km values are not yet known** (see dependencies), so this specification does not
presuppose which way it falls.

## Panel B — the six permutation nulls

| | |
|---|---|
| **X** | separation, shared axis with panel A's Y |
| **Y** | region pair, six rows, ordered by observed separation |
| **Marks** | the null distribution per row as a violin or strip; observed value as a rule crossing it |

Panel B is what makes "not significant" legible as *the observed value sits inside its
null* rather than as an empty space on a chart. It also makes the power problem visible:
the small-n pairs have visibly wider nulls, which is the honest way to show that Central
vs North is undetermined rather than zero.

## Dependencies, and what happens if they are not met

Region centroids come from GADM v4.1 via `scripts/load_geometry.py`. **Neither the GADM
extract nor the loaded geometry is present on this machine** — `provinces.csv` ships with
`centroid_lat` / `centroid_lon` empty by design, and no province currently has a centroid.
Panel A cannot be drawn until that runs.

A *region* centroid is not the mean of its province centroids. It is
`ST_PointOnSurface` of the region's dissolved polygon, matching the province rule in
`load_geometry.py`: a true centroid can fall outside a concave or multi-part shape, and a
Central-region "centre" in the Gulf of Thailand would bend every distance without ever
looking wrong.

**Fallback if geometry stays unavailable:** drop panel A's X axis to an ordinal and render
the figure as a forest plot — pairs on Y ordered by separation, separation on X, null
interval as a horizontal bar, fill by significance. It loses the geographic claim and keeps
everything else. Panel B is unaffected.

## What this costs the paper

RQ1's stated output in §4 is "a number in kilometres: the width of the boundary zone."
**That output does not survive.** The replacement output is a yes/no on regional signal
plus the identification of the single boundary carrying it. §4's RQ1 text needs amending
alongside this figure, or the two documents will disagree.

## Open questions for the researcher

1. **Accept, amend or reject** this replacement. Rejecting is defensible: RQ1 could ship
   without a figure and lean on the table in `docs/rq1_region_level.md`.
2. **Is the geographic axis worth its dependency?** Panel A needs GADM loaded to say
   something the forest-plot fallback cannot. It is the difference between "one boundary
   carries the signal" and "one boundary carries the signal, and it is not the distant one."
3. **Does Figure 2 keep its number?** A figure this different from its specification may be
   better numbered afresh, so that the paper's figure list does not imply continuity with
   a distance-decay analysis that was never run.
